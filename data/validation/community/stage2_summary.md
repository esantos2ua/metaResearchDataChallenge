## Verbatim free text

### Q1 — what would you change about the definition

- **R01** (SIPS): Given the "meta-" prefix, I'd maybe say that metaresearch involves "researching research itself..." which could involve empirical and/or theoretical study. I think there are some theoretical metascientists out there who would balk at the empiricism being baked in.
- **R03** (SORTEE): i would perhaps define what you mean by reproducibility in this? also would it be appropriate to include somewhere a mention of replication?
- **R04** (SIPS): Metaresearch is not necessarily restricted to empirical measures but can also develop theoretical ideas as to causal influences on research.
- **R05** (AIMOS): I wouldn't classify it as purely empirical as I believe theoretical contributions are also made. I'm someone that came from completely outside of this field and haven't collaborated extensively with other metaresearchers, so I'm not well-versed on philosophy of science/STS and whether empiricism is a defining feature of metaresearch relative to these fields. However I do think that while a lot of metaresearch is empirical, there are conceptual contributions in the field. For example, the conceptualisation of paper mines, while based on great empirical studies, is a conceptualisation. I would still call the paper that conceptualised "paper mines" and proposed solutions metaresearch even though it is not empirical https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41636279/
- **R12** (SORTEE): I find the definition broadly acceptable. If anything, I would make explicit that metaresearch concerns empirical studies in which research practices, processes, outputs, or systems are themselves the object of investigation. This may help distinguish metaresearch from studies that simply use research publications or datasets to investigate a substantive scientific question. I would otherwise retain the current concise definition.
- **R13** (AIMOS): I think metaresearch includes more than empirical studies. I think it can include theoretical scholarship, scholarship proposing particular policy solutions, etc.
- **R14** (SORTEE): I guess it can go broader than what is stated, so in a way the definition can limit what meta-research can consider. So maybe just add 'including' before 'its' which then (I think) means it can be these things we list, but also other things.
- **R17** (AIMOS): metaresearch often has a goal of improving practice, and that goal is not captured in the definition. Metaresearch differs from other studies of science (e.g., philosophy or sociology of science) in being 'interventionist', that is, it aims to improve and evaluate deliberate change
- **R18** (Other): Metaresearch is the empirical study of research itself: its practices, processes, products, and systems, including how research is designed, conducted, analyzed, reported, reproduced, synthesized, evaluated, incentivized, and governed
- **R19** (Other): Consider adding historical and social factors to how science is conducted.
- **R20** (SIPS): Empirical? Could it not be theoretical as well?
- **R22** (SORTEE): My questionis more about how to make this term distinct from meta-analyses studies
- **R23** (SIPS): There are data collection, otherwise, this is just epistemology.
- **R25** (SIPS): I would add pedagogy
- **R26** (Other): Would add in science policy (e.g. where would research on peer review fit? Or reaearch integrity)
- **R30** (Other): The definition needs to specify the methods, evaluation, and incentives.
- **R31** (Other): Funding should be mentioned in list.
- **R32** (AIMOS): I would include a sense for how metaresearch has a particular values-driven end goal of scientific reform, which in my opinion distinguishes it from other parallel and complementary research streams that study research as well.
- **R35** (Other): I wonder if "empirical" is an essential word in the defintion. I would say that you can model, simulate some of the processes etc.
- **R36** (SORTEE): To distinguish from science studies, which some would argue is also empirical, it might be worth considering adding something the word 'scientific' - as in, "the empirical scientific study..."

### Q2 — missing domains

- **R02** (SIPS): You might also include studies about the scientific workforce - how many researchers there are, in what disciplines, whether there are shortfalls, how their training is organized. I don't feel strongly about it, but I think there is some of this type of work in metascience.
- **R03** (SORTEE): Research waste?

- **R07** (SORTEE): Research methods: typical study designs, publication types, statistical practices, how is collaboration structured, tools used for bibliography/writing/analysis Research ethics: change in the human and animal ethics landscape: what is allowed, not allowed, how research practices have changed because of ethical considerations Science outside academia: rise of citizen science, how is science communicated to the public, involvements of diverse actors Role of scientific societies
- **R09** (SORTEE): Stevens and Laynor (2023) state the following "As the topics explored within meta-research are broad, to better categorize meta-research efforts, it has been proposed that the discipline be divided into the following themes: methods (how research is performed, e.g., study design, analytic approaches, research ethics), reporting (how research is communicated, e.g., reporting standards, information sharing, conflict of interest reporting), replicability and reproducibility (how research is verified or replicated, e.g., methods of data sharing, efforts to reproduce previous studies), evaluation (how research is evaluated, e.g., peer review, funding criteria, research impact), incentives (how research is rewarded or supported, e.g., promotion criteria, developing research capacity), and organization (how research is organized or categorized, e.g., research categorization, interactions between disciplines) [16]." The theme of methods, particularly "study design and analytical approaches" does not seem to be included in one of the five existing domains. For example, would a systematic review of different approaches to species distribution modeling be considered metaresearch? Or a review of data imputation methods in ecology and evolution? Studies of specific analytical approaches like that would seem to be included under the main umbrella definition but do not clearly fit into one of the five domains.
- **R10** (SORTEE): In addition to QRPs (Research integrity and misconduct), I would probably add (or specify) also statistical practice, publication bias and replicability (replicability of findings, analytical variability). Those topics may fit under research integrity and misconduct, but does not necesiraly involve misconduct. I would also add the research on the research fundings and science policy (funding mechanismes, effects of different policies). Right now the fifth category only cover grant peer review.

### Q4 — reasons or borderline cases for the exclusions

- **R02** (SIPS): There are metascience studies about systematic (and other) types of reviews. For instance, a classic piece is Hilda Bastian's work titled something like "75 reviews a day, how do we keep up." Its about the volume of such work. I would consider that in. There are also studies about research quality that use systematic reviewing type approaches. I would say those should be in.
- **R12** (SORTEE): The distinction is appropriate, but borderline cases may arise where student misconduct overlaps with research training or research practice. The key criterion could be whether the study’s primary object is academic behaviour or the research system itself. Similarly, systematic reviews/meta-analyses should be excluded when they address a substantive question but included when they empirically examine review methodology, reporting quality, reproducibility, or research waste.
- **R13** (AIMOS): The study of student behavior realted to exams and educational requirements is not the study of research or the research pipeline. Applied reviews and meta-analyses could be categorized as research on resesarch but their goal is not to understand research practices, research quality, or the pipeline per se.
- **R25** (SIPS): I think academic integrity can stay if it is looking at the field level.
- **R29** (Other): For students: I agree with the exclusions. However, including research ethics training and misconduct by trainees as the only examples of metaresearch seems too narrow. I and others have examined how students (undergraduate and graduate) and postdocs have implemented data management and open science practices into research projects, theses/dissertations, etc. This is a relatively unexplored area that I feel should be included under the umbrella of metaresearch.

### Q5 — alternative definition of Canadian

- **R02** (SIPS): I wonder about studies that have very large international teams. Would you consider a study with 200 authors and 1 Canadian to be Canadian? I probably wouldn't.
- **R12** (SORTEE): A Canadian study could be defined as a study with at least one author whose bibliographic record lists an institutional affiliation in Canada. This retains affiliation as the basis for classification while making clear that the criterion is applied to the study-level bibliographic metadata rather than author nationality or information requiring full-text assessment.
- **R13** (AIMOS): I'm not sure how to think about this question. It likely depends on the context.
- **R15** (SIPS): Canadian first author OR corresponding author OR Canadian funder (i.e., if any of those are fulfilled = canadian study)
- **R16** (Other): Canadian author or simply canadian data or canada is the subject of the study
- **R21** (SORTEE): I don't like dividing science into national boxes, but if it needs to be done, then for me the primary definition should be about about Canadian funder
- **R22** (SORTEE): conducted in Canada
- **R23** (SIPS): Mostly run in Canada? (whatever the authors are from) A paper with most authors Canadian, even if not first or other "valuable position".
- **R24** (SORTEE): a "Canadian" study should be one whose first or corresponding author is affiliated with a Canadian institution. While the proposed primary definition above is broad and inclusive, it may attribute Canadian status to studies that were conceived, conducted, and led entirely elsewhere, where a Canadian co-author contributed only marginally. Restricting the primary definition to first or corresponding authorship offers a more defensible proxy for Canadian provenance
- **R25** (SIPS): I think it has to be fully Canadian or Canadian lead to be listed as such. Research beyond Canada is simply global research. Research without borders is my preferred method
- **R30** (Other): Canadian can be place of birth, nationality or the nation of an author who affiliated with an Canadian institution, regardless of the author’s nationality.
- **R35** (Other): What about studies of studies conducted in Canada?

- **R13** (AIMOS): In my view, replication studies, multi-analyst projects, registered-report evaluations, preregistration audits and open-science policy studies are all metaresearch. They help us learn about the reliability of research and research practices. Also, studies involving trial registration audits, reporting-guideline adherence, research waste seem like metaresearch to me and not meta-analysis or systematic reviews. I find OpenAlex's classification of those odd.

- **R19** (Other): Some of examples you give (Replication studies, multi-analyst projects) are mostly methods that can be used to answer metascientific questions, e.g. "how replicable is a population of research findings, on average?" but aren't necessarily metascientific if they are not making any claim beyond the specific research question on the topic of the original study (e.g. is this phenomenon replicable? is not a metascientific question, it's just scientific; but "is this field" replicable based on a large scale sample is metascientific). The other research questions (registered-report evaluations, preregistration audits and open-science policy studies) I do think are metascientific: They are asking about how a science policy is being implemented: How well is it being implemented, is it having the expected/intended effects, are there any uninentend costs/benefits to the policy? I do think those are metascientific questions.
