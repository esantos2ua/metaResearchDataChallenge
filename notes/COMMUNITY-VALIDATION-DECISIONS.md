## 0. What we promised respondents

The form description commits us to: log and report every answer; where a community agrees, report
the definition as validated by it; where it disagrees, record the disagreement with its reasoning in
the bias report, add it as a **dashboard sensitivity view where feasible before October**, and
otherwise list it as a **committed change for the next corpus version (v2)**. Each decision below
says which of the three it is.

## 2. Proposed decisions

### Decision 1: Working definition — keep for v1, revise wording for v2
- **Vote:** 27 Agree (75%, 95% CI 59–86%), 2 Unsure, 7 Disagree. Agreement differs by community:
  SORTEE 14/15, SIPS 6/8, Other 6/9, AIMOS 1/4.
- **Dissent:**
  - "Empirical" excludes theoretical, conceptual and modelling / simulation metaresearch
    (6: R01, R04, R05, R13, R20, R35).
  - The list should be open-ended (e.g. add "including") or extended (5: R03, R14, R18, R19, R30).
    Suggested additions include replication, funding and policy.
  - The definition should state the aim of improving research (2: R17, R32).
  - The boundary should be sharper. One suggestion is "the empirical *scientific* study…", to
    separate metaresearch from science studies (R36). Others ask for a clearer line against
    meta-analysis (R22) and against substantive work that uses publications as data (R12).
  - One respondent defends "empirical" as the thing that separates metaresearch from epistemology
    (R23).
- **Proposed:** **KEEP THE DEFINITION UNCHANGED FOR CORPUS v1; COMMIT A REVISED WORDING FOR v2.**
- **Reason:** Retrieval is topic-based, so the wording does not change which works are pulled. It
  does govern coding: the B2 precision sample was coded under the "empirical" wording in
  `docs/CODING_GUIDE.md`. Changing it now would leave the precision estimate measured against a
  definition we no longer publish. Candidate v2 wording, for discussion:
  *"the study of research itself, including its methods, reporting, reproducibility and
  replicability, evaluation, funding, incentives and governance, usually with the aim of improving
  them."*
- **Bias report:** state the 75% agreement, the per-community split, and the theory objection.
  Theory-only papers coded NO in B2 are the ones the objection is about. Adjudicators can count how
  many B2 exclusions were for "not empirical" and report that number.

### Decision 2: The five in-scope domains — validated
- **Votes (is metaresearch):** RDM / open science 36/36; scientometrics 34/36; integrity 33/36;
  publishing 33/36; evaluation / careers / equity 31/36 (86%). There was one "is not" vote, on
  publishing. Evaluation / careers / equity drew the most Unsure votes (5).
- **Proposed:** **REPORT ALL FIVE AS COMMUNITY-VALIDATED.** No change.

### Decision 3: Missing domains — methods and statistical practice is the headline gap
The strongest theme in the free text:

| Theme | n | Respondents |
|---|---|---|
| Methods, statistical practice, QRPs, replicability | 8 | R07, R09, R10, R12, R13, R17, R18, R36 |
| Science policy and research funding | 4 | R10, R17, R26, R31 |
| Evidence synthesis / meta-analysis | 3 | R16, R18, R20 |
| Philosophy / sociology / history of science | 3 | R17, R19, R20 |
| Research training, pedagogy, workforce | 3 | R02, R05, R25 |
| Research waste, efficiency; research ethics | 2 each | R03, R18; R07, R18 |

- **Proposed:**
  1. **Methods and statistical practice → committed v2 change + structural bias in v1.** This is
     the Phase 1 clinical blind spot again, now named by the community across disciplines.
     OpenAlex files audits of power, preregistration, reporting and replication under the
     *substantive* field (R12 says this directly). Our six topics therefore miss them in every
     discipline, not only medicine. For v2: search the OpenAlex topic list for methods and
     reproducibility topics, and test each one against seed papers exactly as T11937 was tested.
     No topic is accepted on the basis of its name alone.
  2. **Science policy and funding → v2 candidate.** Part of it is already covered by domain (e),
     research evaluation. Test for a science-policy topic in the same way.
  3. **Evidence synthesis, research training, HPS → no change.** Record them in the bias report.
     Meta-analyses are excluded by rule 4b, which the community supported (Decision 4). Research
     training is already in scope where it is research-ethics training. Philosophy, sociology and
     history of science sit outside an empirical definition until Decision 1's v2 wording is
     settled.

### Decision 4: The two exclusion rules — validated, with two clarifications for the coding guide
- **4a, classroom academic integrity out:** 34 Agree (94%), 2 Unsure, 0 Disagree. This settles the
  team's 4–3–1 split from Phase 1.
- **4b, applied systematic reviews out:** 30 Agree (83%), 4 Unsure, 2 Disagree.
- **Proposed:** **REPORT BOTH AS COMMUNITY-VALIDATED.** Add two clarifications to
  `docs/CODING_GUIDE.md` for v2. They are not needed for v1, because the B2 coders already applied
  the "stay in" clauses.
  - Studies *of* student research practice, such as data management and open science in theses,
    stay in, and so do field-level studies of integrity (R25, R29). The test is whether the object
    of study is the research system or classroom behaviour (R12).
  - Studies *of* the review literature itself (volume, quality, waste) stay in (R02). This is
    already the intent of the "stay in" clause, but should be said explicitly.

### Decision 5: "Canadian" = any Canadian-affiliated author — retain as primary; add a lead-author view
- **Vote:** 26 Agree (72%, 95% CI 56–84%), 7 Unsure, 3 Disagree. This is the weakest-supported
  item.
- **Dissent:**
  - Large international teams with a single Canadian author are over-counted; respondents prefer
    lead or majority authorship (4: R02, R23, R24, R25).
  - First OR corresponding OR funder (R15).
  - Funder as primary (R21).
  - Canadian data, setting or subject (4: R16, R22, R23, R35), or nationality (R30). These are not
    recoverable from bibliographic metadata, which the question required.
- **Proposed:** **RETAIN "ANY AUTHOR" AS PRIMARY** (same reason as Phase 1 Decision 1), and:
  1. **Sensitivity view before October:** add a *"Canadian lead author (first OR corresponding)"*
     toggle. It needs no new retrieval, because `first_ca` and `corr_ca` are already on every
     record in `records.json`. Corresponding-author coverage is incomplete, so this view is a
     lower bound, as the existing note already says.
  2. **Committed v2 change:** a *"share of Canadian authors"* view, which directly answers the
     "200 authors, 1 Canadian" objection. It needs author counts per work, which are not in the
     current record schema.
  3. Setting, subject and nationality definitions: record them and give the reason they are not
     feasible (they are not in the metadata).

### Decision 6: Blind spots and the cases we were unsure about
- **Registered-report evaluations, preregistration audits and open-science policy studies** are
  metaresearch in every response that mentions them (R13, R19, R29). Treat them as settled.
- **Replication and multi-analyst studies** (R03, R13, R19). R19 makes a useful distinction: they
  are metaresearch when they make claims about a *population* of findings (how replicable is a
  field?), and are ordinary science when they replicate a single finding. Adopt this as the
  coding-guide rule for v2.
- **Record in the bias report as structural blind spots of a topic-based definition:**
  - conceptual papers and editorials with no metaresearch tag (R05);
  - French-language and multilingual work, and Indigenous research governance (R07);
  - evaluation of reform programmes and interventions (R17).
- **Advice:** consult a systematic-review librarian on the v2 topic search (R02). This is cheap, and
  worth doing before the v2 topic tests in Decision 3.
